# Saxon Context Engine — Product & Technical Specification

**From: single-tenant graph prototype → live, multi-source enterprise context platform**

Grounded in the current [`saxon-context-engine`](https://github.com/ananyat24/saxon-context-engine) repository (Python/FastAPI + Neo4j + Graphiti). This spec is written to build *on top of* what's already there rather than replace it — the existing ontology, temporal graph, and tenant-isolation work is real infrastructure, not a throwaway prototype, and the roadmap below treats it that way.

---

## Part 1 — Business-Facing Overview

### The problem

A company's knowledge about its own business lives in a dozen disconnected places: a CRM, an ERP, spreadsheets, email threads, support tickets, Slack messages, maintenance logs. Every one of those systems has its own vocabulary, its own notion of what "the customer" or "the account" means, and its own idea of what's current. When an AI assistant is connected to a business today, it's usually connected to one of these systems at a time, or handed a raw export and left to guess how the pieces fit together. Ask it a question that spans two systems — "who manages the Contoso account, and has anything about their contract changed recently?" — and it either can't answer, or answers confidently from a stale or incomplete slice of the truth.

The failure mode is not "the AI is bad at reasoning." It's that nobody handed it organized, current, sourced facts to reason over. That's a data engineering problem wearing an AI costume, and most companies trying to ship an AI assistant end up solving it badly and expensively, once, per client, from scratch.

### What Saxon does

Saxon sits between a company's scattered systems and whatever is asking questions of them — a chat interface, an agent, another application — and does the consolidation work in between, continuously, so the answer is always built on the current state of the business rather than a one-time export.

Picture a corkboard with pins and string: each pin is a real thing (a person, a company, a machine, an order), each string is a relationship between two things (this person manages this account, this machine sits in this factory). That corkboard is the graph at the center of Saxon. Every pin and string carries a timestamp for when it became true and whether it's still true today, so the graph never just says "Sarah manages the account" — it can say "Sarah managed the account until March, when it moved to Marcus," without ever deleting the older fact. And every pin and string points back to the document, email, or record it came from, so nothing in the graph is an unsourced claim.

Today, getting facts onto that corkboard means manually feeding Saxon a file or a web page. The product this spec describes is the version where Saxon plugs directly into a client's actual systems — their CRM, their database, their email, their document store — pulls from all of them continuously, keeps the graph current as those systems change, and reconciles the same real-world entity when it shows up differently across sources (a "Contoso Ltd" in the CRM and a "contoso.com" domain in email traffic are the same customer).

### The product experience

From the user's side, this stays simple on purpose: ask a question in plain language, get one clear answer. What makes it trustworthy is what sits underneath that answer, one click away — an expandable panel showing exactly which facts the answer drew on, which system each fact came from, when it became true, and whether it has since changed. The answer is the headline; the evidence trail is always there for anyone who wants to check it, without it needing to be read every time. That's the same shape as "the answer plus a citations panel," but built on facts assembled from a graph that already reconciled a dozen systems, not on a pile of retrieved text chunks that may or may not agree with each other.

### Why this is hard to copy quickly

Three things compound here, and they're each real engineering, not marketing claims:

*Temporal correctness.* Most retrieval-augmented systems treat "the account is managed by Marcus" and "the account is managed by Sarah" as two chunks of text that both look relevant and let the model sort it out. Saxon's graph knows one superseded the other and when, so the model is handed the current fact (and, when asked, the history) rather than being asked to referee a contradiction at answer time.

*Vocabulary that adapts without a rewrite.* A hospital, a factory, and a law firm describe similar concepts — an "asset," a "case," a "batch" — completely differently. Saxon's ontology is layered (an enterprise-neutral core, industry packs for healthcare/finance/manufacturing/retail/legal/pharma/sales/supply chain/technology already exist, a per-client extension layer on top), so onboarding a new client's vocabulary is a configuration change, not new application code.

*Sourced, reconciled facts instead of a text dump.* Because ingestion constrains extraction to the ontology's actual entity and relationship types, the graph doesn't accumulate the usual pile of near-duplicate, inconsistently-named nodes that make most knowledge graphs unusable at scale. Every fact keeps a link back to its source, which is what makes the "click to see where this came from" experience possible at all rather than being retrofitted after the fact.

### Who this is for

The ontology domain packs already built (healthcare, finance, manufacturing, retail, legal, pharma, sales, supply chain, technology) point at the intended shape of client: mid-market and enterprise organizations whose knowledge is real but fragmented across systems that don't talk to each other, and who need an AI layer that can answer questions spanning those systems without hallucinating across the gaps. A sales org asking "who owns this account and what's changed" and a manufacturing plant asking "what's this machine's maintenance history and who's responsible for it" are the same underlying product.

### Where things stand today, in plain terms

What exists now proves the hard part of the idea works: text goes in, gets turned into structured, timestamped, sourced facts, and comes back out through a question-answering API with per-client data isolation. What's missing is breadth and automation — today, getting data in means manually pointing Saxon at a file or a single web page; nothing yet reaches into a live CRM, database, or inbox and keeps itself current on its own. Everything in Part 3 is about closing exactly that gap, in order, without re-doing the parts that already work.

## Part 2 — Technical Architecture & Stack

### Layered architecture

The system is organized in the same shape as the reference architecture this spec was asked to align with (source layers → orchestration → context graph → context API → consumers), mapped onto Saxon's own terms:

```
 STRUCTURED IQ        DOCUMENT IQ           LIVE IQ              WEB IQ
 (CRM/ERP/DBs via     (email, PDFs,        (CDC + webhooks,     (already built:
  connectors)          tickets, Slack)      push notifications)  app/ingestion/web_source.py)
        \                   |                    |                    /
         \__________________|____________________|___________________/
                                       |
                         INGESTION & EXTRACTION LAYER
              (per-source connector -> normalize -> prose ->
               ontology-constrained LLM extraction -> dedup/content-hash)
                                       |
                          CONTEXT ORCHESTRATION LAYER
                (app/context/: Connect · Resolve · Reconcile ·
                       Plan · Rank · Assemble · Deliver)
                                       |
                              CONTEXT GRAPH (Neo4j)
              Entities · Relationships · Temporal validity (Graphiti) ·
              Evidence/provenance · Source authority · Ontology types ·
                    Org/authorization subgraph (:User, REPORTS_TO)
                                       |
                          CONTEXT API  +  MCP SERVER
                 (FastAPI /api/v1/context/query, tenant-scoped)
                                       |
                 Saxon chat UI  ·  Claude/Copilot/agents via MCP  ·
                         client apps via API key
```

Everything below the "Ingestion & Extraction" line already exists in the repo in working or partially-working form. Everything above it (the four source-type lanes) is the main build-out this spec describes.

### Token & cost efficiency, concretely

Each of these is either already partly built or a natural extension of something that is:

1. **Ontology-constrained extraction** (already built). Passing the ontology's actual entity/relationship types into extraction means the model isn't burning tokens (or accuracy) inventing types — the repo's own README documents this concretely: unconstrained extraction on the same data invented six of eight relationship types.
2. **Content-hash dedup before extraction** (already built for the web connector, in `app/api/connectors.py`). A sync that fetches identical content skips extraction entirely rather than paying for a near-duplicate episode. Generalize this to every connector type, not just web.
3. **Small-model, capped-token synthesis** (already built, `_SYNTHESIS_MAX_TOKENS = 80`). A single-fact answer skips the synthesis LLM call entirely; multi-fact answers get a tightly bounded call. Extend the same discipline to any new synthesis paths.
4. **Query planning to avoid running every retriever on every query** (planner exists as a stub, not yet implemented). The graph retriever alone answers most "who/what/when" questions; semantic search and live-data lookups should only fire when the planner judges the graph result insufficient, not unconditionally.
5. **Prompt caching for static, repeated context.** The ontology schema passed into every extraction call, and any per-tenant system prompt, is large and identical across many calls. Anthropic's prompt caching (as of 2026: cache writes at roughly 1.25x base input cost, cache reads at roughly 10% of base input cost) turns a repeated ontology schema from full-price-every-time into a one-time cost amortized across a session or an ingestion batch. This is a direct, mechanical win with no quality tradeoff — worth wiring in as soon as extraction moves to Claude for a given tenant.
6. **Batch processing for non-time-critical ingestion.** Historical backfills and large one-time imports don't need synchronous, real-time extraction — Anthropic's Batch API (roughly half the per-token cost) is a straightforward fit for "ingest this client's last two years of CRM history" style jobs, as opposed to live incremental syncs.
7. **Semantic response caching.** Two different users (or the same user twice) asking near-identical questions shouldn't re-run retrieval and synthesis from scratch. A cache keyed on a normalized/embedded form of the query, scoped per-tenant and invalidated when relevant facts change, avoids repeat spend on repeat questions.
8. **Hybrid search over a separate vector database.** Graphiti's own hybrid search (semantic + BM25 + graph traversal) removes the need to stand up and pay for a separate vector store for semantic retrieval — one less system, one less place tokens/cost leak.

### Compatibility notes and explicit v0-overwrite flags

Where full compatibility with the current stack would cost quality or features, this is called out as a deliberate later-version break rather than quietly working around it forever.

- **`anthropic<1.0.0` pin — the overwrite condition below has now actually happened.** `graphiti-core`'s bundled `AnthropicClient` calls `messages.create(..., temperature=...)` in a shape that breaks under the Anthropic Python SDK's 1.x changes. **The production deployment's `LLM_PROVIDER` is `anthropic`, not `gemini`** — Claude is the live default extraction/synthesis provider today, which is exactly the condition this note originally said would make the pin due for a real fix. It hasn't been fixed yet: either wait for an upstream `graphiti-core` fix, or write a thin custom `LLMClient` for Graphiti that calls a current-version Anthropic SDK directly, bypassing the bundled client. This is no longer a someday item — it's a live, unaddressed dependency constraint on the production path.
- **Neo4j Community + `group_id` tenancy.** Correct, working, and honestly documented in the repo as "a reasonable baseline, not the strongest possible guarantee." **Overwrite note:** for any client with real compliance requirements (healthcare/pharma clients in particular, given the existing ontology packs), plan a per-tenant Neo4j database (Aura Enterprise or self-hosted Enterprise) migration rather than trying to harden the shared-database model further — the repo's own README already reaches this conclusion.
- **Synchronous, click-to-sync connectors.** Deliberately simple for the current single-connector-type MVP (documented in `app/api/connectors.py`'s own comments as "not a background job or a schedule... real future scope, not this"). **Overwrite note:** this model is fully replaced, not extended, once live/continuous ingestion (v2 below) lands — it becomes a scheduler + queue, and the "sync now" button becomes an optional manual trigger on top of that, not the primary mechanism.
- **Per-tenant Gemini free-tier keys — the risk this note warned about is now directly demonstrated, not hypothetical.** Every tenant's embeddings still run on Gemini regardless of `LLM_PROVIDER` (Claude has no embeddings API of its own — see `graphiti_adapter.py`), so switching extraction/synthesis to Claude does *not* remove this dependency. Ingesting three real connectors' worth of Solandra data in one session hit both the **per-minute** free-tier embedding quota (100 requests/min) and, separately, the **per-day** quota (1000 requests/day) — the latter fully exhausted mid-session, blocking further ingestion until a second, fresh Gemini key was swapped in. No backoff/retry exists for this today; a rate-limited sync just errors. Treat Gemini-per-tenant as the "trial tier" default, not the production default, for any tenant with real, continuous ingestion volume — this is now a proven operational failure mode, not a theoretical one.

None of these require touching working code today — they're flagged so the roadmap in Part 3 doesn't accidentally build v2/v3 features on top of an assumption (single connector type, sub-1.0 Anthropic SDK, free-tier rate limits) that's already known to need replacing.

## Part 3 — Versioned Implementation Roadmap

Each version lists its goal, concrete deliverables, and exit criteria (how to know it's actually done, not just started). Versions build strictly on the previous one; nothing here proposes re-doing work that already exists and works.

### v0 — Current state (baseline, already built)

**Goal:** none — this is what exists today and what everything else builds on.

Ontology (core + 9 domain packs + customer extension) validated and tested. Neo4j persistence and Graphiti temporal-fact tracking implemented and tested. File- and single-web-page ingestion working, ontology-constrained. Graph-based retrieval working; semantic and live-data retrieval stubbed. Context assembly produces a plain-text summary with source facts; structured `ContextPacket` fields mostly unpopulated. Multi-tenant API-key auth, `group_id` isolation, org-hierarchy-scoped visibility, and local LLM spend caps all implemented. Document Sets and web-page Connectors (single type) implemented. Basic chat frontend, deployed to Azure Container Apps against Neo4j Aura.

### v0.5 — Hardening & connector SDK groundwork

**Goal:** make the current single connector type (`web`) into a real, pluggable pattern before adding more types to it, so v1 adds connectors instead of also redesigning the abstraction.

- Generalize `app/graph/connectors.py`'s `Connector` model and `app/api/connectors.py`'s dispatch (`_SUPPORTED_TYPES`) into a small connector interface: `fetch() -> list[NormalizedRecord]`, `content_hash()`, `source_description()` — same shape `web` already has, made explicit and reusable.
- Generalize the content-hash dedup-before-extraction pattern (currently web-connector-only) so every future connector type gets it for free.
- Resolve or work around the `anthropic<1.0.0` pin if Claude is going to be used anywhere in the ingestion path for testing this milestone (see compatibility notes above) — don't defer this past the point where it blocks real work.
- Add integration tests for the connector interface itself (not just the web connector), so new connector types have a contract to test against.

**Exit criteria:** a new connector type can be added by implementing the interface and registering it in one dispatch table, with no changes to `IngestionPipeline`, ontology handling, or the API route.

### v1 — First live connectors, scheduled sync

**Goal:** prove ingestion from a real structured source and a real unstructured source, on a schedule, not just on manual click.

- Add one structured connector (a client's Postgres/MySQL database, or a CRM's REST API) using the v0.5 interface, converting rows to prose via the existing `structured.py` pattern.
- Add one unstructured connector (Gmail or Microsoft Graph for email) with basic attachment/body text extraction.
- Replace "sync now" as the *only* mechanism with a scheduler (APScheduler is enough at this stage — no need for Celery/RQ yet) running incremental syncs on a per-connector interval, tracking a cursor (e.g., `updated_at` watermark or Gmail history ID) so each run only pulls what's new.
- Extend `record_sync_result`/connector status tracking to surface last-sync timestamps and errors in the (still basic) frontend, so a client can see their connectors are alive.

**Exit criteria:** two live source types stay current in the graph without a human clicking "sync," each only re-processing genuinely new/changed data, within the existing per-tenant spend budgets.

**Status:** `database`, `documents`, and `email` connector types exist and work end to end (`app/ingestion/database_source.py`, `document_source.py`, `email_source.py`). Scheduling is built: `app/graph/connector_scheduler.py` runs every tenant's connectors on an interval (`CONNECTOR_SYNC_INTERVAL_MINUTES`, default 15) via APScheduler's `AsyncIOScheduler`, started/stopped from `app/main.py`'s lifespan — the manual "Sync now" route and the scheduler both call the same extracted `run_connector_sync()` (`app/ingestion/connector_sync.py`), so they can't drift apart. Runs in-process per container instance; a real multi-instance deployment should move this to a dedicated worker (see v2's "worker pool" step). `app/api/connectors.py`'s `_connector_health()` flags a connector "stale" once it's gone more than 3x the sync interval without a success, since nothing else would otherwise surface "the background scheduler silently stopped working for this one" — an amber badge plus an aggregate "N connectors need attention" line in the frontend.

**Follow-ups, done:**

- **Per-connector file upload**, not one shared hardcoded demo folder: `POST /connectors/{connector_id}/files` (`app/api/connectors.py`) writes to that connector's own `data/uploads/<connector_id>/` folder (`connector_id` server-generated, uploaded filename reduced to its basename before touching disk — no path traversal). `DatabaseConnector` reads its own upload folder if populated, falling back to the bundled demo dataset otherwise; a dropped-in CSV with no hand-picked spec gets one inferred from its header (`_infer_spec`, one CSV per record type). Later extended (see below) to the `email` connector type too, for a JSON message export. Frontend: the file picker now lives directly in the "New source connector" form (`CONNECTOR_UPLOAD_TYPES` in `frontend/app.js`), not hidden behind first creating the connector.
- **Per-knowledge-base UI scoping**: once a tenant had more than one knowledge base under the same API key, the "Source connectors"/"Document sets" tables and the "Ask" scope dropdown showed *every* knowledge base's connectors/sets regardless of which tab was selected (`GET /connectors`/`GET /document-sets` are tenant-scoped, not KB-scoped, and the frontend rendered the full response unfiltered). Never a query-answer leak (every query has always been `group_id`-scoped), but broke the "this tab is its own instance" feel. Fixed client-side: `connectorsForCurrentScope()`/`docSetsForCurrentScope()` filter what's *displayed* per the active KB tab.
- **Entity resolution given an owned home**: `_extract_candidate_entities`/`_extract_lowercase_word_candidates`/`_extract_id_candidates`/`_normalize_entity_name` and the exact/normalized/CONTAINS matching chain moved out of `GraphRepository` into `app/graph/entity_resolution.py`, with real logging (`logger.debug` on every match decision) and its own tests — `GraphRepository`'s own methods are now thin delegating wrappers. Same pass added `_infer_date_column` (`app/ingestion/database_source.py`): an auto-inferred CSV spec now detects a real date column instead of every episode collapsing to ingestion-time `now()` as its `reference_time`.
- **Graphiti's own contradiction-detection gap, pinned not fixed**: ingesting a Solandra ERP `day1` then `day2_update` snapshot (same records, several fields changed) showed some transitions tracked correctly and others not (a shipment's "held for inspection" → "released" status change never invalidated the old fact) — confirmed not a bug in this codebase (both episodes' generated text genuinely differed), but in `graphiti_core`'s own LLM-driven edge dedup/contradiction judgment treating two very-similar sentences as the same fact. `tests/test_graphiti_contradiction_gap.py` reproduces this directly against real Neo4j/LLM and is marked `xfail(strict=False)` — a silent expected-fail today, an unmissable XPASS signal if Graphiti ever gets more reliable at this. Excluded from the default run (costs real LLM spend); run explicitly with `pytest tests/test_graphiti_contradiction_gap.py -m real_llm`.
- **One-click "Connect Google Drive" and a JSON-upload path for `email`** (Gmail/Outlook exports), plus a real production incident and its fixes — all documented under v1.5 and this section's newest follow-up below, since they're connector-UX work that landed after the rest of this version was already "done."

**Follow-up, done — real production incident: a connector's preview showed another connector's facts, root-caused and fixed with new admin recovery tooling:** clicking a connector to see "what's been pulled into the graph from this source" (`GET /graph/nodes`/`/graph/relationships`) only ever filtered by `knowledge_base` (`group_id`) — so a knowledge base fed by more than one connector (the normal case once a client has several sources) showed every *other* connector's facts too, not just the one clicked. Fixed by tagging the `:Episodic` node each ingested record produces with `connector_id` (`app/ingestion/connector_sync.py`, retried 3x with backoff after a transient Neo4j blip was observed in production to hit this write specifically — content_hash still gets recorded on the content that really was ingested regardless, so a missed tag on a since-unchanged connector could never be recovered without a forced re-sync) and adding an optional `?connector_id=` filter to both graph routes (`app/api/graph.py`) that joins through it. A connector synced *before* this fix has old, untagged facts that a plain re-sync can't retroactively tag (content-hash dedup skips re-ingesting unchanged content, so "Sync now" alone is a no-op here) — the connector preview's empty state now says so explicitly and offers a **"Fix attribution"** button that actually works (purges the connector's own tagged data via the new `DELETE /connectors/{id}/data`, which also resets its `content_hash`, then forces a real re-sync), instead of the wrong "just click Sync now" advice it originally shipped with. `DELETE /connectors/{id}/data` (`purge_connector_data`, `app/graph/connectors.py`) is itself real incident-recovery infrastructure, not a one-off script: a `RELATES_TO` fact is only fully deleted when *every* episode touching it belongs to the target connector (one another connector or an earlier sync also touched keeps existing, just with that connector's episode stripped from its `episodes` list) — used live to clean up real mock-data pollution (see v5's status below) and reset connectors for a clean re-sync. A related admin-only route, `POST /admin/connectors/{id}/reassign-tenant` (`app/api/admin.py`), moves a connector's *management* ownership to a different tenant without touching its already-ingested data (which is `group_id`-scoped, not `tenant_id`-scoped) — built to consolidate connectors created under a throwaway tenant (see v5's status below) into the tenant that should actually own them, without re-ingesting. All covered by real-Neo4j tests (`tests/test_graph_connector_scope.py`, `tests/test_purge_connector_data.py`, `tests/test_admin_connector_reassign.py`, `tests/test_connector_sync.py`'s retry-recovery case).

**Operational gaps found live in production, not previously known, still open:**
- **Uploaded files don't survive a container restart.** Azure Container Apps gives each restart a fresh replica with wiped ephemeral disk (`"ephemeralStorage": "2Gi"`, ephemeral by name) — a `database`/`email` connector's uploaded source files vanish, and its next sync silently falls back to the bundled demo dataset (or, if that connector type has no fallback, fails) with nothing telling the operator why. Bit this deployment three times in one incident. No persistent volume is wired up for `data/uploads/`. Worth fixing before any client relies on upload-based connectors in production — either a mounted persistent volume, or moving uploads into Neo4j/blob storage instead of local disk.
- **The background scheduler races with manual syncs on shared quota**, with no lock preventing the same connector being synced twice concurrently (scheduler tick + a manual "Sync now" close together) and no backoff once Gemini's free-tier rate limit is hit — it just keeps burning remaining quota and erroring. `scheduler_lock.py`'s Neo4j-backed lease lock prevents two *replicas* from double-scheduling, but nothing prevents a scheduled tick and a manual trigger from overlapping on the same connector within one replica.

### v1.5 — Unified connector platform for broad source coverage

**Goal:** stop hand-writing a connector per client system — this is the step that makes "any and all data sources" actually tractable and cost-effective rather than an ever-growing bespoke-integration backlog.

- Stand up Nango (self-hosted, open-source) as the source-of-record for OAuth, polling/webhook scheduling, and per-source normalization across its ~900 prebuilt API connectors (CRM, ERP, HRIS, accounting, documents, email, and more).
- Write one adapter layer that takes a Nango-normalized record and hands it to the same `structured.py`/prose-conversion + `IngestionPipeline` path every other source already uses — Nango replaces "who do I poll and how do I auth," not "how does this become a graph fact."
- Keep the v1 hand-written connectors as-is for any source Nango doesn't cover, or where a client requires direct, non-Nango access for compliance reasons — this is additive, not a rip-and-replace.
- Re-run the v0.5 connector-interface tests against a Nango-backed connector to confirm the abstraction held.

**Exit criteria:** onboarding a new client system that already has a Nango connector is a configuration/mapping change, not new integration code.

**Status — deliberate substitution:** standing up Nango itself (a separate hosted service, plus per-client OAuth app registrations) was judged too much new infrastructure to take on before a single real live connector had been proven end to end. Instead, `google_drive` (`app/ingestion/google_drive_source.py`) was built as one real, direct connector — no mock data, a genuine live external source — authenticating as a Google Cloud **service account** (`GOOGLE_DRIVE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_JSON`, see `.env.example`) rather than per-user OAuth, since a service account needs no interactive consent flow and is what a server-side "Sync now"/scheduled sync actually needs. It only ever sees folders explicitly shared with its own email address. Reads plain text, Markdown, CSV, PDF, Word (`.docx`), and Google Docs/Sheets/Slides (via `pypdf`/`python-docx` for the binary formats — a scanned/image-only PDF has no text layer to extract and is silently skipped, not OCR'd). A second real connector, `sharepoint` (`app/ingestion/sharepoint_source.py`), followed the same pattern: authenticates as an Azure AD (Entra ID) app registration via the OAuth2 client credentials flow (`SHAREPOINT_TENANT_ID`/`CLIENT_ID`/`CLIENT_SECRET`), reads a site's default document library through Microsoft Graph, and shares the same PDF/DOCX/plain-text extraction code with Drive (factored out into `app/ingestion/document_text_extraction.py` once a second connector needed it). One real difference worth flagging, not glossing over: Drive's access boundary is per-folder (share explicitly, or the service account sees nothing); SharePoint's is a single org-wide Graph `Sites.Read.All` application permission — once admin-consented, this connector type can read *any* site in the tenant, not just the one a connector happens to point at. This satisfies v1.5's underlying goal ("prove a real live source works, cheaply") without its literal Nango deliverable. **Overwrite note:** Nango (or an equivalent unified platform) is still the right answer once *breadth* of sources matters more than proving the pattern once — don't read this substitution as "Nango isn't needed," just "it wasn't the next-cheapest proof." `google_drive`'s own interface (`SourceConnector`) is exactly what a future Nango adapter would also implement, per v0.5's exit criteria, so nothing here needs to be redone to add Nango later.

**Follow-up, done — a second, genuinely one-click Drive connect option, alongside (not replacing) the service account:** the service-account path above needs a folder shared with an operator-managed email address first — real friction for a client who just wants to connect their own Drive. Added `google_drive_oauth` (`GoogleDriveOAuthConnector`, same file): a real per-user OAuth consent flow — click "Connect Google Drive," sign in via a popup (Google Identity Services), pick specific files via the Google Picker, done. Deliberately scoped to `drive.file` (Google's narrowest Drive scope: the app can only ever read files a user explicitly picked) rather than the broader `drive.readonly` a folder-level grant would need — `drive.readonly` is a *restricted* scope requiring Google's app-verification process and, at real scale, a paid third-party CASA security audit; `drive.file` needs neither. The real tradeoff, disclosed in the UI: a file added to the "same" folder later isn't auto-included — reconnecting and re-picking is required. OAuth refresh tokens are Fernet-encrypted at rest (`app/graph/token_crypto.py`, a new required `TOKEN_ENCRYPTION_KEY` — no plaintext fallback), never returned in any API response, and revoked at Google's end when the connector is deleted. New settings: `GOOGLE_OAUTH_CLIENT_ID`/`_SECRET` (`.env.example`, wired into `scripts/deploy_azure.sh`).

### v2 — Continuous, low-latency ingestion

**Goal:** move from "polled on a schedule" to "reflects change in near real time" for sources that need it, and decouple capture from extraction so ingestion spikes don't spike LLM spend.

- Add a change queue (Redis Streams, or SQS if the deployment target is AWS) between "something changed" and "run extraction on it" — every connector (Nango webhook, CDC event, scheduled poll) writes to the queue rather than calling extraction inline.
- Add Debezium-based CDC for structured databases where a client needs sub-minute freshness and has granted direct database (logical replication) access — distinct from the Nango/API-polling path, for the subset of sources where that level of freshness matters.
- Add webhook-based push where the source supports it (Nango's own webhook relay, Gmail/Graph push notifications) rather than polling everything.
- Add a worker pool consuming the queue with rate limiting and batching, tuned against the existing per-tenant/per-bucket spend caps.

**Exit criteria:** a change in a connected live source appears in the graph within the target latency for that source type (seconds for webhook-driven sources, minutes for CDC/polled sources), and ingestion volume spikes don't produce a corresponding spike in concurrent LLM calls.

**Status — the queue/worker-pool bullet shipped at in-process scope; CDC and real webhook push not started (need infrastructure this repo can't stand up on its own).** `app/graph/ingestion_queue.py`'s `IngestionQueue` is a real "capture and extraction are decoupled" implementation, just scoped to what this deployment's actual traffic needs: an in-process `asyncio.Queue` plus a small semaphore-bounded worker pool (2 concurrent jobs by default), not a hosted broker. It fixed a genuine bug along the way, not a hypothetical one: `POST /api/v1/connectors/{id}/sync` used to block the whole HTTP request until fetch *and* extraction finished, which was a real risk for a large Drive/SharePoint folder (verified live locally: the sync route now returns `202 {"queued": true}` immediately, the connector's status flips to `"queued"` right away, and the actual Gemini extraction calls happen seconds later, confirmed in the server log, entirely after the HTTP response was already sent). The scheduled sync path (`connector_scheduler.py`) still calls `run_connector_sync` directly rather than through the queue -- it was already sequential/self-rate-limited (one tenant's one connector at a time, `max_instances=1`), so there was no real spike to fix there; the queue's actual job is protecting the manual "Sync now" path, where several connectors' syncs really can get triggered close together. **Explicit substitution, not a shortcut:** a true Redis Streams/SQS-backed queue is still the right answer once this app runs more than one replica concurrently (each instance would otherwise have its own queue -- functionally fine, since `run_connector_sync`'s content-hash dedup still prevents double-ingestion, but not a shared work queue) -- `IngestionQueue`'s small `enqueue`/`start`/`stop` interface is deliberately what that swap would replace, so nothing above it needs to change later. Debezium CDC and real webhook-based push (Google Drive push notifications, Microsoft Graph webhook subscriptions) are genuinely not started -- both need external, source-side registration (a verified webhook domain, subscription renewal management) that's real setup work for whoever owns those source accounts, the same category of blocker Nango was deferred for in v1.5. This in-process queue is the actual prerequisite for that work when it happens: a webhook receiver has to ack fast and can't block on an LLM call, which is exactly what this now lets it do.

**Follow-up, done — the scheduler's own multi-replica gap, closed without standing up a separate worker process:** `connector_scheduler.py`'s docstring already named this: this deployment's Azure Container Apps config genuinely scales 0-3 replicas, and each replica's in-process APScheduler would independently tick the same tenants' connectors on the same interval -- redundant (not unsafe; `run_connector_sync`'s content-hash dedup still prevented double-ingestion), but N replicas meant N times the outbound calls to every connector's source and N redundant reconciliation passes (see v2.5's follow-up below). Rather than the "real" fix this version's own bullet list names (a separate dedicated worker), added `app/graph/scheduler_lock.py`: a distributed lease lock backed by Neo4j (every replica already has a connection to it -- no new infra dependency), using a uniqueness-constrained `:SchedulerLock` node so a MERGE-based acquire is safe under real concurrent racing, not just sequential calls that happen to look concurrent (verified with a real multi-threaded test against the real driver, not just monkeypatched calls). `_tick` acquires the lock (lease = one full sync interval, so a replica that dies mid-tick strands it for at most one missed cycle) before doing any real work; a replica that doesn't win it skips the tick entirely. Covered by `tests/test_scheduler_lock.py` (acquire/renew/expire/release, and the concurrent-race case) and two new cases in `tests/test_connector_scheduler.py` (`_tick` skips real work when the lock isn't acquired, runs it when it is).

**Investigated, no action taken — Plan/Rank/Assemble/Deliver, audited the way Resolve/Reconcile were:** checked whether these later pipeline stages have the same "no owned home, scattered logic, real bugs traced back to that" problem Resolve had before its own extraction (see v2.5's follow-up and CLAUDE.md's Reconcile follow-up). They don't: `app/context/orchestrator.py`'s own module docstring already documents that a standalone `ContextPlanner`/`Ranker`/`Composer` were scaffolded early, never wired into the real pipeline, and deliberately removed rather than kept as misleading placeholders once it was clear the real logic already lived elsewhere -- `GraphRepository.search_graphiti_facts`'s resolve-first-fall-back-to-search-second decision *is* Plan; `ContextOrchestrator._apply_authority_tie_break` *is* Rank; `ContextOrchestrator.get_context_packet`/`get_causal_context_packet` (dedup, transition-finding, causal-chain assembly) *is* Assemble; `app/context/query_service.py` (scope resolution, caching, the actual HTTP/MCP-facing response) *is* Deliver. Each already has its own docstrings explaining real production bugs it was written to fix (not hypothetical ones), `logger.warning`/`logger.exception` on its own failure paths, and dedicated test coverage (`tests/test_orchestrator_observability.py`, `test_causal_recommendation.py`, `test_source_authority.py`, `test_query_service_observability.py`). Forcing an extraction here -- the way entity_resolution.py was pulled out of graph_repository.py -- would repeat the exact mistake this codebase already made once and reverted: renaming working, tested, documented code into new files doesn't fix anything when there's no actual gap to close.

### v2.5 — Cross-source entity resolution & reconciliation

**Goal:** close the specific gap the repo's own roadmap already identifies — Graphiti's semantic dedup is good within a source's own vocabulary, but doesn't reliably know a CRM's `contact_id: 4471` and an email connector's `sarah@contoso.com` are the same person unless something tells it so.

- Add an explicit "Reconcile" step in the ingestion path (matching the reference architecture's own naming) that runs deterministic identity matching where a real key exists (email address, external system ID, domain) before an episode reaches Graphiti.
- Only reach for probabilistic matching (e.g., a library like Splink) where no deterministic key exists — most cross-CRM/ERP/email matching in practice does have one.
- Store the resolved cross-source identity as part of the entity's evidence/provenance, so "this is the same Sarah Chen across three systems" is itself a sourced, inspectable fact, not a silent merge.

**Exit criteria:** a person or organization that appears across two or more connected sources resolves to one graph entity with multiple sourced identifiers, verifiable by inspecting that entity's evidence.

**Status — MVP-scoped reconciliation shipped, full version deferred:** `GraphRepository._match_entities_by_name`/`_resolve_named_entities`/`search_graphiti_facts` now reconcile an *exact* (case-insensitive) name match across every connector in a query's scope — e.g. "Fenwick & Cole Legal" mentioned in both a CRM record and a separate email now pools facts from both, rather than only whichever node happened to be picked first. This is deterministic, real reconciliation, but on a weaker signal than the spec's own plan (normalized name equality, not a shared key like an email address or external system ID — no such shared key exists in the current mock/live data yet). The known tradeoff: two genuinely different entities sharing an exact name would incorrectly merge. Loose (CONTAINS) matches are deliberately NOT reconciled this way, to avoid re-introducing the padded-results problem an earlier fix removed. Revisit with a stronger signal (e.g. a real shared identifier field) once one exists in the data, per this version's original plan.

**Follow-up, done — Reconcile is now a first-class, persisted stage, not just a live query-time computation:** checked every bundled/demo connector dataset before building this (see this version's own status note above) and confirmed the finding still holds: no connector's data carries a real shared external key across another connector (mock_crm's AccountID never appears in mock_email's free text). So `app/graph/reconciliation.py` builds a *stronger name* matching tier rather than an ID-matching path with nothing real to match against -- if a future connector's data does carry a real shared key, that's a strictly stronger tier to add above this, not a rewrite. Two tiers: (1) exact/normalized cross-connector name match (the same equality `match_entities_by_name` already did live) is confident enough to auto-merge, now persisted as a `:SAME_AS` edge instead of recomputed every query; (2) a new fuzzy-similarity tier (close but not normalized-equal, e.g. a typo) is NOT confident enough to auto-merge -- it's written as a `:ProposedMerge {status: pending}` node a human has to approve or reject via `GET/POST /api/v1/reconciliation/...` (approving writes the `:SAME_AS` edge; rejecting marks it decided so it's never re-proposed) and a new "Entity matches to review" panel in the frontend. Runs once per connector sync across the whole tenant's group_ids (`app/ingestion/connector_sync.py`, best-effort -- a reconciliation failure doesn't fail the sync itself), idempotent on repeat runs (checks existing `:SAME_AS`/`:ProposedMerge` pairs first). Query-time reads (`GraphRepository._match_entities_by_name`/`_resolve_named_entities`) now expand through `:SAME_AS` on top of their own live matching via `reconciliation.expand_same_as`, so an approved fuzzy merge grounds a query the same way an exact/normalized one always has -- scoped to the caller's own `group_ids`/`visible_uuids` even though `:SAME_AS` itself spans a whole tenant, so an expansion can never surface a fact from outside what that particular query was authorized to see. Covered by `tests/test_reconciliation.py` (tiering, idempotency, same-group_id exclusion, approve/reject, the group_id-scope-leak case). Verified live in the browser against the local dev deployment: seeded a fuzzy-matching pair, approved it through the actual UI, confirmed the `:SAME_AS` edge landed in Neo4j with `confidence: 'fuzzy_approved'`.

**Follow-up, done — two real bugs found in a security/correctness review of the above, both fixed:** (1) `approve_proposal` used to `SET status = 'approved'` and only then try to write the `:SAME_AS` edge as a second, separate query -- if either named entity had since been deleted (its connector removed, its data re-synced away), that second query silently matched nothing while the first had already committed `'approved'`, leaving a proposal marked approved with no edge ever created. Now one statement matches both entities and sets status together, so either the whole thing commits or the proposal is left untouched (still `'pending'`, so it can be retried once the data's back). (2) Both `_create_same_as` and `_create_proposal` used `CREATE`, not `MERGE` -- the in-memory "already paired" check `reconcile_tenant` uses to skip redundant work is only a snapshot taken when that particular call started, so two different connectors of the same tenant syncing close together (a realistic case: the background scheduler and a manual "Sync now" overlapping) could each run `reconcile_tenant` concurrently and both decide the same pair needs linking, creating a duplicate `:SAME_AS` edge or a duplicate `:ProposedMerge` for the same pair (the latter especially bad -- approving one sibling wouldn't clear the other, leaving an orphaned pending item in the review queue forever). Fixed with the same idiom `scheduler_lock.py` already established: `_create_same_as` now `MERGE`s the relationship pattern itself (Neo4j's own atomic find-or-create for a specific edge between two specific nodes); `_create_proposal` now `MERGE`s on a new deterministic `pair_key` property (sorted-uuid-pair) under a uniqueness constraint (`ensure_reconciliation_indexes`). Both fixes verified with real multi-threaded tests against the real driver (`tests/test_reconciliation.py`'s `test_approve_proposal_fails_cleanly_if_an_entity_was_deleted_first` and `test_concurrent_reconcile_runs_never_duplicate_a_same_as_edge_or_proposal`), not just reasoned about -- the concurrency test failed against the pre-fix code (3 proposals from 6 racing threads instead of 1) before the `MERGE`s were added.

### v3 — Token-efficient query layer & the answer/evidence UI

**Goal:** this is where the product experience described in Part 1 (one answer, expandable full sourcing) actually ships, and where per-query cost gets actively minimized rather than just not-yet-a-problem.

- Implement `app/context/planner.py` (currently a stub) as a cheap up-front classifier deciding which retrievers a given query actually needs, so semantic/live-data retrieval only runs when the graph retriever's result is insufficient.
- Implement `SemanticRetriever` on Graphiti's built-in hybrid search (semantic + BM25 + graph traversal) rather than standing up a separate vector database.
- Extend `ContextPacket` so the API returns structured, per-fact source attribution (which connector/document/message, what timestamp, current vs. superseded) rather than only a flattened text summary — this is the actual data the frontend's "expand for sources" panel needs.
- Wire in Anthropic prompt caching for the ontology schema and any per-tenant system prompt used in extraction/synthesis, for tenants on the Claude provider path.
- Add a semantic response cache (query → recent answer, scoped per tenant, invalidated on relevant graph changes) to avoid repeat retrieval+synthesis spend on repeat questions.
- Update the frontend to render the answer with a click-to-expand sources section, driven by the extended `ContextPacket`.

**Exit criteria:** a query's cost is visibly lower for a question the graph retriever alone can answer, and every answer in the UI can be expanded to show the exact facts, sources, and timestamps behind it.

**Status:**
- The planner + semantic-search bullets turned out to already be substantially met by the existing design, just organized differently than this plan assumed: `GraphRepository.search_graphiti_facts()` already tries named-entity resolution first and only falls back to Graphiti's built-in hybrid search (semantic + BM25 + graph traversal) when that doesn't resolve — which *is* the "only run semantic search when the graph result is insufficient" decision a separate `ContextPlanner` would have existed to make, and *is* "semantic search on Graphiti's hybrid search, not a separate vector database." The early-scaffolded `app/context/planner.py`, `app/context/ranker.py`, `app/context/composer.py`, `app/retrieval/semantic_retriever.py`, and `app/retrieval/live_data_retriever.py` (never wired into the real pipeline, verified via a repo-wide import search) were removed rather than kept as misleading placeholders — see `app/context/orchestrator.py`'s module docstring for the real architecture.
- Per-fact source attribution is done: every fact returned by `search_graphiti_facts` now carries `group_id` (which connector/knowledge base it actually came from), threaded straight through to the API response and shown in the frontend as a small "from X" tag next to each fact — only when a Document Set (multiple possible sources) is the active scope, since it's redundant noise on a single-connector query.
- Anthropic prompt caching is done: `app/graph/caching_anthropic_client.py`'s `CachingAnthropicClient` subclasses graphiti_core's `AnthropicClient` to send the system prompt as a `cache_control`-marked block (graphiti_core sends it as a plain string, which can never be cached) and is now what `llm_provider="anthropic"` actually builds. The local spend limiter (`app/graph/spend_limiter.py` via `graphiti_adapter._apply_spend_limit_anthropic`) was updated in the same change to count `cache_creation_input_tokens`/`cache_read_input_tokens` toward estimated spend — those aren't part of the plain `input_tokens` figure, so leaving them out would have made the spend estimate silently wrong the moment caching turned on.
- A response cache is done: `app/context/response_cache.py`'s `ResponseCache` caches `(tenant, group_ids, as_user, normalized query, num_results) -> ContextPacket` in-process, with a TTL (`response_cache_ttl_seconds`, default 5 min — shorter than the connector sync interval) as a safety net, plus explicit invalidation the moment a connector sync actually changes data for a group (`run_connector_sync` calls `invalidate_group()`), so a stale answer doesn't linger for the full TTL right after a real sync. This is exact-match caching (normalized whitespace/case), not embedding-similarity "near-duplicate question" matching — the spec's literal "semantic" framing would need a vector-similarity lookup instead of a dict key, which is real, separate scope if two *differently-worded* questions need to share a cache hit.
- The click-to-expand frontend sources section is DONE in spirit (the bulleted fact list with current/superseded badges and now per-fact source tags), not built as a separate collapsible "expand for sources" affordance layered on top of the answer.

### v3.5 — Expose the context graph as an MCP server

**Goal:** this is the literal "Context API/MCP" layer from the reference architecture — make a client's consolidated graph queryable by any MCP-capable agent (Claude Desktop, Claude Code, Copilot, a custom agent), not only through Saxon's own chat UI.

- Stand up an MCP server (Graphiti already ships a reference implementation to build from) wrapping the same tenant-scoped, authorization-aware query path `/api/v1/context/query` already uses — same API key and `as_user` scoping, no separate auth model.
- Expose it as a small number of well-described tools (e.g., "query this client's context graph," "look up an entity's current facts and history") rather than raw Cypher access, keeping the ontology-and-authorization guarantees intact.
- Document the MCP connection for a client the same way `X-API-Key` is documented today.

**Exit criteria:** Claude (or another MCP client) can be pointed at a tenant's Saxon instance and answer questions grounded in that tenant's graph, with the same tenant isolation and role-based visibility the HTTP API already enforces.

**Status:** Done, mounted into the same FastAPI app/process as the HTTP API (one container, one deploy) via the official `mcp` Python SDK (`mcp>=2.0.0` -- note the SDK's own 2.0 rename of `FastMCP` to `MCPServer`, `mcp.server.mcpserver.MCPServer`). The HTTP route's own query logic was extracted into `app/context/query_service.py::execute_context_query()` so the HTTP route (`app/api/context.py`) and the MCP server (`app/mcp/server.py`) both call the exact same scope-resolution/cache/orchestrator path rather than risk drifting. Two tools are exposed: `query_context_graph` (the same ask-a-question-get-sourced-facts path `/api/v1/context/query` uses, including the response cache) and `list_available_sources` (a tenant's own knowledge bases and document sets, so an agent can discover what it's allowed to query before guessing an id). Auth is the same `X-API-Key` header the HTTP API uses, read from the raw MCP request headers via `Context.headers` and checked against the same `settings.tenant_api_keys` lookup `require_tenant` uses -- not a separate auth model, and a missing/unknown key is rejected before any tenant data is touched. One MCP-specific wrinkle worth flagging: the SDK masks any exception a tool raises as a generic "Error executing tool X" unless it's specifically an `mcp.server.mcpserver.exceptions.ToolError` (or `HTTPException`, translated to one) -- both the auth failures and scope errors (e.g. an unknown `document_set` id) are raised as `ToolError` specifically so the calling agent sees the actual reason, not a generic crash message. Reachable at `POST https://<deployment>/mcp` (streamable HTTP transport); routed directly onto the main app's router rather than via `app.mount()`, since mounting at `/mcp` 307-redirects to `/mcp/` and not every MCP client follows a redirect on a POST.

**Goal:** the items already explicitly flagged as "not needed yet, but known to be needed eventually" throughout this spec and the repo's own README.

**Exit criteria:** a new enterprise client can be onboarded, isolated, billed, and monitored without operator intervention beyond initial setup.

**Status — observability bullet only, MVP-scoped:** the "which retriever ran and why, and per-query cost" half of this bullet is done; per-connector sync health was already done earlier (see v1's status note on `_connector_health()`), and the admin-plane/billing/per-tenant-database bullets remain not started. `ContextOrchestrator.get_context_packet()` (`app/context/orchestrator.py`) now classifies every answer's `metadata.retrieval_path` as `"entity_resolution"` (resolved a named entity directly, never called Graphiti's paid hybrid search), `"semantic_search"` (fell back to it), or `"none"` (nothing matched) -- this *is* the already-existing planner decision from v3's status note, just finally surfaced instead of only ever happening invisibly. `app/context/query_service.py` adds `metadata.cache_hit` (true/false) and `metadata.cost_usd` -- a per-query cost estimate from diffing `SpendLimiter.spent("query")` before/after the orchestrator call, `null` (not a misleading `0.0`) for Gemini-provider tenants since only the Anthropic/Azure OpenAI paths are wired to the spend limiter at all (see `app/graph/graphiti_adapter.py`). ### A note on sequencing

The order above is deliberate: v1/v1.5 (breadth of sources) come before v2 (real-time freshness) because a live connector to one source is more valuable to prove the model than sub-second latency on that same one source. v2.5 (entity resolution) comes after v2 (continuous ingestion) rather than before, because reconciliation only becomes a visible problem once multiple live sources are actually flowing into the same graph. v3 (token efficiency + UI) comes after ingestion breadth is real, because optimizing query cost against a graph that only has one manually-ingested file in it doesn't tell you anything about production cost. This ordering can compress — v1 and v1.5 in particular could run together if a first client's needs point straight at Nango-covered systems — but skipping straight to v3/v4 features on top of v0's manual-ingestion-only graph would mean building the token-efficiency and MCP layers against a shape of data the real product will never actually have.

## Part 4 — Pivot: Context Graph / Context Layer / Context Engine (leadership-directed)

Leadership wants this framed and built as three explicitly named layers, matching a reference architecture rather than this repo's own earlier vocabulary. This section maps that vocabulary onto what already exists (nothing was renamed in code for this) and records the new work it required.

### Vocabulary mapping

- **Context Graph** — the enterprise understanding: entities, relationships, metrics, semantics, provenance, authority, permissions, temporal state. Maps to Neo4j + Graphiti's bi-temporal fact tracking, `ontology/`, `app/graph/authorization.py` (the org/permissions subgraph), and the new `source_authority` field on `:Connector` (below).
- **Context Layer** — the reusable contract presenting common business meaning to BI tools, Copilots, agents, and apps alike, decoupled from source-specific schemas. Maps to the ontology's core-plus-domain-pack model and the `SourceConnector` abstraction every source is normalized through before extraction.
- **Context Engine** — the runtime: resolve intent → retrieve → reconcile → apply permissions → assemble governed context. Maps to `app/context/query_service.py` / `app/context/orchestrator.py` / `app/graph/graph_repository.py`'s retrieval path — now with a second, causal-reasoning mode alongside the original fact-only one (below).

This pivot didn't require touching that mapping's shape — the gap was two genuine capabilities the reference architecture calls for that the runtime didn't have yet (causal reasoning, BI as a consumer), plus one ontology gap (`sales`/`supply_chain` were empty scaffolds despite being advertised domain packs) and one small provenance addition (source authority). See below for what was actually built.

### Ontology gap: `sales` and `supply_chain` populated

**Status: done.** `ontology/domains/sales.yaml` and `ontology/domains/supply_chain.yaml` were empty stubs (`entities: {}`) despite being two of the nine advertised domain packs, and despite being literally the domains leadership's own illustrative example (an at-risk Order → Product → Component → Supplier → QualityEvent) lives in. Populated both, every type `extends:`-ing an existing core type per the manufacturing pack's own pattern:

- `sales.yaml`: `Account`/`Contact`/`SalesRep` (extend `Organization`/`Person`), `Lead`, `Opportunity` (extends `Process`), `Quote` (extends `Document`), `Order` (extends `Transaction`), `SalesTerritory` (extends `Group`), plus the relationships connecting them (`OWNS_ACCOUNT`, `PURSUING`, `CONVERTED_FROM`, `RESULTED_IN_ORDER`, `ORDERED_BY`, `ASSIGNED_TERRITORY`, `COVERS_ACCOUNT`, `QUOTED_FOR`, `WORKS_AT`).
- `supply_chain.yaml`: `Supplier` (extends `Organization`), `Component` (extends `Asset`), `BillOfMaterials` (extends `DataObject`), `Warehouse` (extends `Location`), `InventoryItem` (extends `Resource`), `PurchaseOrder`/`Shipment` (extend `Transaction`), `QualityEvent` (extends `Issue`) — plus `SUPPLIES`, `COMPOSED_OF`, `LISTED_IN`, `STOCKED_AT`, `ORDERED_FROM`, `SHIPPED_VIA`, `DELIVERED_TO`, `FLAGGED_BY`, `QUALITY_ISSUE_ON`, chosen specifically so the leadership example is representable end to end: `Order` -RESULTED_IN_ORDER/ORDERED_BY-> `Account`, `Product` -COMPOSED_OF-> `Component` -SUPPLIES(inverse)-> `Supplier` -FLAGGED_BY-> `QualityEvent`.

One judgment call worth flagging (not blocking, but a design decision made without a design-review round-trip since it was cheap to get right and easy to revise): both packs originally had an alias `po` (sales meant "Order", supply_chain meant "PurchaseOrder") — `OntologyRegistry.register()` merges alias keys as a flat namespace across every domain pack with last-loaded-wins, so this was a real, silent collision risk, not a hypothetical one. Resolved by dropping `sales.yaml`'s `po` alias (Order is still reachable via "order"/"customer"/"deal" etc.) rather than inventing a scoping mechanism aliases don't have today.

**Follow-up, done — the underlying class of bug fixed, not just this one instance:** `OntologyRegistry.register()` (`app/ontology/registry.py`) previously let *any* scalar-valued key (in practice, only `aliases` — every other section's entries are dicts, merged by updating properties) silently overwrite on a collision between ontology files, with no warning. It now raises `OntologyValidationError` the moment two registered files define the same alias to two different values, instead of resolving the ambiguity implicitly by load order — the exact bug class the `po` collision above was one instance of. A repeated alias that two packs agree on identically still registers fine (not a conflict); the dict-merge behavior for entity/relationship/event/fact types (two packs both touching `Organization`, say) is unchanged. Covered by `tests/test_ontology.py`'s `test_registry_rejects_two_packs_aliasing_the_same_word_to_different_types` and a regression test (`test_all_bundled_ontology_files_register_without_conflict`) that registers every shipped domain pack together under the stricter check.

Verified live: `python -c "from app.ontology.bootstrap import registry; ..."` and the deployed `/ui` ontology panel both show all 57 entity types / 78 relationship types including the new ones, with no validation errors.

### v5 — Causal reasoning + governed recommendations

**Goal:** answers that go "what happened → why → impact → recommendation" by chaining across a relationship path, not just restating facts a single retrieval pass already found — while keeping that inference-capable mode strictly separate from the existing fact-only path (`ContextOrchestrator._synthesize_answer` stays exactly as fact-only as it always was; nothing here loosens it).

- A bounded causal-chain retriever: once entity resolution finds a relevant node, walk a few hops over the ontology's own causal relationship types (`DEPENDS_ON`, `CAUSED_BY`, `AFFECTS`, `RESULTED_IN`, `SOURCED_FROM` — already in `ontology/core.yaml`) rather than only the entity's own directly-touching edges.
- A recommendation-synthesis pass over that assembled chain, returned in its own `metadata.recommendation` field — never blended into `metadata.summary`.
- Record any recommendation as a `Decision` entity (already defined in `ontology/core.yaml`, previously unused) so it's an auditable graph fact, not a throwaway string. Saxon does **not** act on it — recommendation only, execution is explicitly out of scope.

**Exit criteria:** a query naming a relevant entity gets back a fact-grounded chain, a visually/structurally distinct generated recommendation, and a queryable `:Decision` node — without the plain `/query` path's behavior changing at all.

**Status: done.** `GraphRepository.causal_chain_for_query`/`_causal_chain_facts_from` (`app/graph/graph_repository.py`) resolve the query to a starting entity the same way `search_graphiti_facts` does, then walk up to 4 hops over exactly the five causal relationship types above (`WHERE ALL(rel IN relationships(p) WHERE rel.name IN $causal_types)`), capped at 25 facts, respecting the same `visible_uuids` role-based filter every other retrieval path does. `ContextOrchestrator.get_causal_context_packet` (`app/context/orchestrator.py`) is a deliberately separate method (not a mode flag on `get_context_packet`) that synthesizes a `_CausalRecommendation` (`what_happened`/`why`/`impact`/`recommendation`) via a system prompt explicitly permitted to infer from how facts connect — the one LLM call in this codebase allowed to do that, everywhere else stays fact-only by design. A produced recommendation is written as a real `:Entity:Decision` node (`app/graph/decisions.py`'s `record_decision`, linked to the anchor entity via `INVOLVES` — `Decision extends Event` and core already defines `Event -INVOLVES-> Entity`) — best-effort (a logging failure doesn't fail the query). Exposed as `POST /api/v1/context/query/causal` (`app/api/context.py`) and the MCP tool `query_causal_chain` (`app/mcp/server.py`), both calling the same `execute_causal_query` (`app/context/query_service.py`) — deliberately **not** going through the response cache, since recording a Decision is a real side effect a cache hit would silently suppress. Frontend: a separate "Explain why + recommend" button next to (not blended into) "Ask", rendering the recommendation in a visually distinct block with its own `Decision` audit-id note.

Judgment call worth flagging: max hops (4) and the fact cap (25) were chosen to match the spec's own worked example's length (Order→Product→Component→Supplier→QualityEvent is 4 hops) plus headroom, not derived from a real client's graph density — worth revisiting once a real causal query against real production data shows whether that's too tight or too loose.

**Follow-up, done:** two gaps from the first pass closed without changing the mode-separation design above. (1) `execute_causal_query` (`app/context/query_service.py`) now tracks `metadata.cost_usd` the same way `execute_context_query` does — diffing `SpendLimiter.spent("query")` before/after the orchestrator call, `null` for non-cost-tracked (Gemini) providers rather than a misleading `0.0`; a causal query's cost was previously left out of the response entirely. (2) `execute_causal_query` now accepts `as_user`, resolved into `visible_uuids` via `authorization.resolve_as_user`/`get_visible_entity_uuids` the exact same way `execute_context_query`'s single-knowledge-base branch does — previously the causal path ignored org-hierarchy-scoped visibility entirely regardless of what a tenant had configured, which was a real gap against this app's own stated authorization model, not just an unimplemented nice-to-have. `document_set` remains unsupported for this mode (a causal chain still needs one clear knowledge base to write its `Decision` node into) — that part of the original scoping decision didn't change. Both the HTTP route (`POST /api/v1/context/query/causal`) and the MCP tool (`query_causal_chain`) now take an `as_user` argument.

**Follow-up, done — relevant-fact fallback when there's no causal chain:** in practice, none of the currently-ingested demo data (Northwind, mock CRM/docs/email, manufacturing, legal) actually produces edges typed `DEPENDS_ON`/`CAUSED_BY`/`AFFECTS`/`RESULTED_IN`/`SOURCED_FROM` — so "Explain why" always fell through to "no causal chain found," even for a question a direct, non-causal fact could answer (e.g. "where was Order 10252 shipped?" against a `LOCATED_AT` edge). Fixed without loosening what counts as a causal chain: `get_causal_context_packet` (`app/context/orchestrator.py`) now falls back, only when the causal-typed walk finds nothing, to the anchor entity's own direct facts of any relationship type (`GraphRepository.direct_facts_for`, a public wrapper around `_entity_own_facts`) — answered the exact same fact-only way the plain Ask path already does (`_synthesize_answer`). No recommendation is generated and no `:Decision` is recorded from this fallback; `retrieval_path` reports `causal_fallback_direct_facts` so it's distinguishable from a real causal chain in telemetry. The nuance "don't surface an irrelevant relationship" comes for free from `_synthesize_answer`'s existing behavior — it's told to answer the *question*, so an irrelevant direct fact (e.g. a `LOCATED_AT` edge when asked who requested the order) stays out of the synthesized answer while still appearing in `metadata.facts`; verified live against real data for both directions (a location question picks the location fact, a "who requested" question picks the requester fact, from the same two-fact set). Covered by three new tests in `tests/test_causal_recommendation.py`.

**Follow-up, done — root cause of "Explain says the exact same thing as Ask" identified and fixed (frontend only, no backend change):** the fallback above reuses `_entity_own_facts` -- the *identical* underlying data `search_graphiti_facts`'s entity-resolution branch already uses for the plain Ask path -- so whenever an entity has no causal-typed chain (true for most entities outside the supply-chain shape: people, companies, plain attributes), "Explain why + recommend" was drawing from the exact same facts Ask draws from, with **no visual or textual signal that this had happened**. `frontend/app.js`'s `runCausalQuery` rendered the `!rec` case as a single bare `<p class="muted">` with no evidence list at all, identical in effect to what Ask already showed for a single-fact entity (e.g. "Vantus Robotics operates in the Industrial Automation industry" from both panels, verbatim) -- reasonably read as a bug even though the underlying data was correct, since nothing distinguished "the causal engine ran and found nothing beyond this fact" from "the causal engine never really engaged with this question at all." The signal to fix this already existed in the API response (`retrieval_path: "causal_fallback_direct_facts"`) but the frontend never branched on it. Fixed: that specific `retrieval_path` value now renders with an explicit disclaimer ("No causal chain connects this to anything else -- here's the most directly relevant fact instead (not an inference, not a recommendation)") *and* a full `renderFacts` evidence breakdown (current/superseded badges, same as Ask) underneath it -- previously the fallback showed zero evidence structure at all. `retrieval_path === "none"`/`"causal_chain_empty"` (nothing resolved, or resolved but genuinely isolated) keep the plain muted-paragraph treatment, since there's no fact-list to show either way. Verified against the real "Vantus Robotics" response from production (7 direct facts, one superseded) -- renders the disclaimer, the synthesized answer, and a correctly badged evidence list, no longer indistinguishable from Ask's own rendering. No backend change: `metadata.summary`/`metadata.facts` stay pure fact-content (consistent with the plain Ask endpoint's own semantics), and `retrieval_path` is the framing signal any consumer (this UI, a future one, an MCP client) can build appropriate copy around.

**Follow-up, done — the frontend fix above treated a symptom; the actual root cause was a missing case in the backend.** Re-tested against a real two-entity question ("How is Industrial Automation connected to Diego Alvarez?") and the frontend disclaimer fix wasn't enough -- the *content* itself was wrong, not just unlabeled: the answer was "Vantus Robotics operates in the Industrial Automation industry," a fact that doesn't mention Diego Alvarez at all. Root cause: `causal_chain_for_query` (`app/graph/graph_repository.py`) never implemented a two-named-entity case at all -- unlike `search_graphiti_facts`, which has always had a dedicated branch for "how is X connected to Y" (`_relationship_path_facts`, the shortest connecting path). The causal-mode resolver's own docstring said outright "only ever anchors on the first resolved candidate" -- so a query naming two entities silently discarded the second one, anchored on whichever entity happened to resolve first (regex/candidate ordering, not query semantics), and returned *that* entity's own unrelated direct facts as if they were an explanation of a connection. Not a framing problem -- a wrong-answer bug that traceability/evidence UI alone couldn't fix, because the underlying fact genuinely had nothing to do with the question asked.

Fixed properly, not patched around:
- New `GraphRepository._relationship_path_full_facts`: the causal-mode counterpart to `_relationship_path_facts`, but returning full fact dicts (`relationship_type`, `is_valid`, hop order) instead of bare strings, and `group_id`-scoped the same way `_causal_chain_facts_from` is (same reasoning as that earlier security fix, applied consistently here).
- New `GraphRepository.is_entirely_causal(facts)`: true only when every fact's `relationship_type` is one of the ontology's causal types. The single-anchor walk is causal-typed by construction already; a two-entity connecting path is NOT type-restricted the same way (it needs the *real* shortest path, whatever it's made of, same as `search_graphiti_facts`'s own two-entity branch) — this is what decides whether an assembled path is a genuine causal chain worth inferring a recommendation from, or a plain topological connection that should be reported as fact-only, fully-sourced evidence instead. Never loosens what counts as causal for the recommendation call itself -- it only ever gates toward the *more* conservative, fact-only path.
- `causal_chain_for_query`'s return signature changed from `(anchor, facts)` to `(anchor, second_entity, facts)` -- `second_entity` is set only when two named entities resolved, and `facts` becomes the connecting path in that case rather than one entity's own edges.
- `ContextOrchestrator.get_causal_context_packet` (`app/context/orchestrator.py`) now branches on both dimensions: two entities vs. one, and `is_entirely_causal` vs. not. A genuinely causal two-entity path gets the full recommendation-synthesis-and-`:Decision` treatment, same as the single-anchor case always has; a non-causal two-entity path (like the Diego Alvarez case -- `IS_A`/`MANAGED_BY` edges, not causal types) gets the fact-only, fully-evidenced treatment (factored into a new shared helper, `_fact_only_causal_packet`, used by both this and the existing single-entity direct-facts fallback) instead of a fabricated recommendation. No path at all between the two entities reports "No connection found between X and Y," mirroring `search_graphiti_facts`'s own phrasing for the identical case, rather than a misleading unrelated fact.
- Frontend: the disclaimer-and-evidence rendering from the fix above already covers this correctly, since it triggers on "no recommendation but real facts exist" rather than one specific `retrieval_path` string -- the new `causal_path_between_entities` value gets its own, more accurate disclaimer wording ("No single causal chain explains this -- here's the actual connection between them instead"), but needed no new rendering logic.

Verified live against the real, unfixed production bug before deploying the fix (confirmed it reproduces exactly as diagnosed: `retrieval_path: "causal_fallback_direct_facts"`, an answer that never mentions the second named entity), then against 6 new real-Neo4j tests in `tests/test_causal_chain.py` (a fully-causal two-entity path, a mixed-type path matching the real bug shape, no-path, cross-`group_id` path exclusion, plus `is_entirely_causal` itself) and 3 new orchestrator-level tests in `tests/test_causal_recommendation.py` (causal two-entity → real recommendation + Decision; non-causal two-entity → fact-only with both hops as evidence, no Decision; no-path → correct "not found" message naming both entities). Full suite: 266 passed.

**Follow-up, done — a `:Decision` node was leaking into normal Ask/Explain answers and suggested questions.** Found for real in production, from actually exercising the feature the way a user would: after a causal query about "CX-17 Power Relay" had run once and recorded a `:Decision` (working exactly as designed -- see `record_decision`), a *later*, unrelated question about the same entity started returning garbage: the answer was literally "Saxon generated this recommendation while analyzing: Why is the CX-17 Power Relay affected?" (the Decision's own boilerplate `INVOLVES`-edge text), and a suggested-question chip read "What's changed recently about Recommendation for: Why is the CX-17 Power Relay affected??" (the Decision node's own auto-generated `.name`, sampled as if it were a real entity). Root cause: `record_decision` creates a node labeled `:Entity:Decision` (the ontology models `Decision extends Event extends Entity`), so every general-purpose "give me this entity's facts" / "give me real entity names" query in the codebase -- `_entity_own_facts`, `_match_entities_by_name`, `_relationship_path_facts`/`_relationship_path_full_facts`, `GET /graph/nodes`/`/relationships`/`/summary`, `authorization.py`'s equivalent `as_user`-scoped functions -- treated a Decision node exactly like real business data, with nothing anywhere excluding it. Not a display bug -- the underlying data returned to every consumer (this UI, a future one, an MCP client) was wrong.

Fixed by adding an explicit `NOT ...:Decision` (or `NONE(node IN nodes(p) WHERE node:Decision)` for the multi-hop path queries) to every one of those query sites -- a `:Decision` node is an internal audit record, never something a person is asking about or a fact worth surfacing about something else. Deliberately left the OData feed (`app/api/odata.py`) untouched -- a BI consumer building an audit-trail report over generated recommendations is a legitimate use for that surface, unlike the narrative-answer UI. Existing Decision nodes already in the graph are untouched too (they're meant to be a permanent record -- the fix stops them from being *retrieved* as if they were regular facts, not the record itself from existing). Covered by three new real-Neo4j tests in `tests/test_decision_isolation.py`, each reproducing one part of the real bug: a Decision's `INVOLVES` fact never shows up in `direct_facts_for` for the entity it's about; a Decision's own auto-generated name never resolves as a query anchor (while the real entity name embedded within it still correctly does -- this one caught a wrong assumption in the test's first draft, since the Decision's name literally contains the original query, which itself names the real entity); and the causal-chain walk never routes through a Decision node.

**Follow-up, done — the per-query cost estimate showing up in the main UI.** `metadata.cost_usd` (an estimate of this app's own LLM spend, not something relevant to what an end user asked) was shown right under every answer -- reasonably flagged as exposing internal operating cost to every tenant's user, not a "nice to have" observability detail. Removed from `renderQueryStats`'s visible line entirely (still in the raw API response for anyone building their own tooling against it -- this is a display-only removal, not a backend change). Replaced with a real, admin-gated running total instead of just deleting the visibility outright: a small `GET /api/v1/admin/spend` route (`app/api/admin.py`), gated by the same operator-only `ADMIN_API_KEY`/`require_admin` every other admin route already uses (no new auth concept invented), reporting both spend-limiter buckets' real persisted totals (`app/graph/spend_limiter.py`) and their configured budgets. Surfaced via a small, low-emphasis link in the page footer ("Admin: view running spend") that prompts for the admin key once (stored in `localStorage` under its own key, `saxon_admin_key`, separate from the tenant access key) and shows the totals on click -- not a real admin panel, just enough to keep this visible to whoever operates the deployment without putting it in front of every end user. Covered by two new tests in `tests/test_admin_spend.py`.

**Follow-up, done — Google Drive connector failing on real files with a misreported mimeType:** a folder of genuinely-supported files (plain CSVs, in the case that surfaced this) could sync and fail outright with "No supported files found in that Drive folder," with no visible reason in the UI. Root cause: Drive's own `mimeType` field for a regular (non-Google-native) file isn't always reliable -- depending on how a file got into the folder (drag-drop from certain OS file managers, Drive for Desktop sync, some third-party upload tools), a genuine `.csv` can come back tagged `application/octet-stream` instead of `text/csv`, and `google_drive_source.py`'s `_fetch_file_as_record` checked `mimeType` only, with no fallback -- so every file in an otherwise-valid folder looked unsupported at once. `sharepoint_source.py` already has to work around the equivalent Graph API quirk (its own comment: "Graph sometimes reports a generic/blank mimeType... extension is checked as a fallback"); Google Drive's connector didn't have the same fallback. Fixed by checking file extension (`.txt`/`.md`/`.csv` for plain text, `.pdf`/`.docx` for the binary parsers) alongside `mimeType`, not instead of it -- mirrors the existing SharePoint fix exactly. Covered by three new tests in `tests/test_google_drive_connector.py`: a CSV and a PDF with a generic mimeType now read correctly via the extension fallback, and a genuinely unsupported file (`.png`, wrong mimeType, no matching extension) still correctly fails the sync.

**Follow-up, done — a sync error had no visible explanation in the UI:** `record_sync_result` has always captured the actual error message server-side (`connector.last_error`, already returned by `GET /connectors`) -- the "Error" badge just never exposed it beyond a hover-only `title` attribute, easy to miss and useless on touch devices. The badge is now a real `<button>` (`.badge-btn` in `styles.css`) that shows `last_error` in a popup on click, still keeping the hover title for desktop users who don't need to click. This is what actually surfaced the Google Drive root cause above -- clicking the error badge is how "No supported files found in that Drive folder" became visible at all.

**Follow-up, done — pre-push security review finding:** `_causal_chain_facts_from`'s multi-hop MATCH constrained only the anchor node's uuid and the relationship types, with no `group_id` check on any node along the up-to-4-hop path — unlike every other relationship traversal in this codebase (`app/graph/authorization.py`, `app/api/odata.py`'s `list_facts_odata`), which scope both ends of an edge explicitly. In practice a `RELATES_TO` edge shouldn't naturally span two knowledge bases (Graphiti's own `group_id` partitioning), but that was an unenforced assumption on the one deep, externally-reachable, multi-hop traversal in the codebase — and a causal answer also gets written into a permanent, auditable `:Decision` node, so a future data-quality bug or entity-merge feature crossing that boundary wouldn't just answer one query wrong, it'd leave a record. Fixed by adding `AND ALL(node IN nodes(p) WHERE node.group_id IN $group_ids)` to the Cypher, with `group_ids` threaded through from `causal_chain_for_query`. Covered by a new regression test, `test_causal_walk_never_crosses_into_another_groups_node` in `tests/test_causal_chain.py`, which creates a causal edge from an anchor into a different group's node and asserts the walk returns no facts.

**Follow-up, done — real per-fact traceability to the actual source document, not just the knowledge base:** the "Where this answer comes from" evidence list only ever said which knowledge base a fact came from (`group_id`, shown as a KB label, and only when a document set spans several), never which document/row inside it -- so two facts from entirely different CRM rows or web pages looked identically sourced. This is exactly what real per-fact provenance data already existed for and was never surfaced: Graphiti stores an `episodes` property directly on every `RELATES_TO` edge (the uuids of the ingested Episodic node(s) whose extraction produced or last touched that fact), and every Episodic node carries the `source_description` set at ingest time (`app/ingestion/file_source.py`'s `SourceRecord`, e.g. `"orders.csv (Order)"` for a structured row, a URL for a web page). New `GraphRepository._resolve_episode_sources` batch-resolves a fact's `episodes` uuids to their `source_description`s in one query (not one per fact), wired into every fact-building path that returns full fact dicts: `_entity_own_facts`, `_relationship_path_facts` (upgraded from bare fact strings to full dicts so the plain-Ask "how is X connected to Y" case gets sources too, not just the causal-mode equivalent), `_relationship_path_full_facts`, `_causal_chain_facts_from`, and the semantic-search branch of `search_graphiti_facts` (Graphiti's own `EntityEdge` search results already carry `episodes` natively — no extra query needed to get at it, just to resolve it). Every fact dict now carries a `sources: list[str]`. Frontend: `renderFacts` (`frontend/app.js`) now shows each fact's actual source document(s) as its own tag, unconditionally (the existing KB-label tag stays document-set-only, since it's redundant noise on a single-connector query) — this is the real answer to "I want real context WITH THE SOURCES... traceability and explainability," not just a UI relabeling.

Also fixed the literal duplication in "Explain why + recommend"'s fact-only fallback: when exactly one fact backs the answer, `_fact_only_causal_packet` (`app/context/orchestrator.py`) sets `summary` to that fact's own text verbatim (`lines[0] if len(lines) == 1`), so the frontend was printing the identical sentence twice — once as a bare paragraph, once again as the sole bullet in the evidence list below it, with nothing distinguishing them. That's the "the answer, where it comes from, and the explain say the same thing" complaint. Fixed by only rendering the summary paragraph when there's more than one fact (i.e. `summary` is actually LLM-synthesized across them, not a verbatim echo) — with a single fact, the evidence list (now carrying its real source document) is the only thing shown, since it already says everything the paragraph would have. Covered by 5 new real-Neo4j tests in `tests/test_fact_sources.py` (`_resolve_episode_sources` itself, direct facts, the causal chain path, the plain-Ask two-entity path, and the no-episodes-recorded edge case). Full suite: 276 passed.

**Follow-up, done — the fact-only fallback's summary paragraph still read as repetitive even across several facts, and a separate, worse retrieval bug was found alongside it:**

1. On this dataset's scale, a small handful of facts stitched into one LLM-synthesized sentence reads as a near-restatement of the same facts listed right below it — the single-fact case above was the sharpest example, but the multi-fact case wasn't meaningfully better, just less literal. Per explicit direction ("if not [genuinely a bug], just cut this piece"): the causal fact-only fallback (`runCausalQuery` in `frontend/app.js`) no longer renders `metadata.summary` at all, regardless of fact count — just the disclaimer and the sourced evidence list. The API/MCP response still carries `metadata.summary` unchanged (the `query_causal_chain` MCP tool's docstring documents it as part of its contract for a different kind of consumer, one that isn't reading a page next to a bulleted list) — this is a UI-only simplification, not a backend behavior change.

2. Real bug, found from an actual query: "what do we know about diego" (typed casually, lowercase) returned Diego Alvarez's two real facts padded out with several unrelated orders/shipments/quality events, while the identical question typed as "Diego Alvarez" resolved cleanly to just those two facts. Root cause: `_resolve_named_entities`'s candidate extraction (`app/graph/graph_repository.py`) requires a capitalized, multi-word phrase (`_PROPER_NOUN_RE`) or an id-shaped phrase (`_extract_id_candidates`) — a plain lowercase name matches neither, so it was never even attempted against the graph, and the query fell straight through to Graphiti's own hybrid search, which (per its own long-standing, well-documented behavior in this codebase) has no relevance threshold and pads out the answer with whatever else scores similarly. Not a ranking/synthesis problem — the entity that should have grounded the answer was never looked up at all.

   Fixed with a new, deliberately lenient fallback, `_extract_lowercase_word_candidates`: when a query has no capitalized-phrase candidate at all, every remaining word (3+ characters, common English filler words excluded via a `_STOPWORDS` set) is tried through the exact same resolution pipeline proper nouns already use (`_match_entities_by_name`'s exact → normalized → CONTAINS chain) — so it still only ever resolves to a name that's actually in the graph, this just changes which words get a chance to try. Deliberately scoped narrowly: only runs when `proper_nouns` is empty (a properly-capitalized query, including a two-entity one, is untouched and pays no extra cost), and — like the existing id-phrase candidates — a word here can never trigger the hard "no entity matching that name" short-circuit on its own, so an ordinary open-ended question with no real entity in it still falls through to normal search rather than a false "not found". Covered by 4 new pure-function tests in `tests/test_candidate_extraction.py` and 2 new real-Neo4j tests in `tests/test_entity_reconciliation.py` (a lowercase query resolves precisely and excludes an unrelated entity in the same group that a broad search would have padded in; a genuinely generic lowercase query with no real entity still falls through to search rather than short-circuiting). Full suite: 282 passed.

**Follow-up, done — the real reason "Explain why" still just repeated the evidence list even for an on-topic question, this time a genuine causal-recognition bug, not a data/fallback issue:** re-reported after all the fixes above ("the explain why button still just repeats where this answer comes from"). Root cause: `GraphRepository._CAUSAL_RELATIONSHIP_TYPES` was a hardcoded Python list of only core.yaml's 5 generic causal types (`DEPENDS_ON`/`CAUSED_BY`/`AFFECTS`/`RESULTED_IN`/`SOURCED_FROM`) — but `supply_chain.yaml` (see v2's own status note: "chosen specifically so the leadership example is representable end to end") defines its *own* relationship names for that exact chain (`SUPPLIES`, `COMPOSED_OF`, `FLAGGED_BY`, `QUALITY_ISSUE_ON`), none of which were in that hardcoded list. So the reference architecture's own worked example — Order → Product → Component → Supplier → QualityEvent — could never produce a real causal answer even with perfectly-extracted data; `is_entirely_causal` would see none of those relationship names as "causal" and every such chain fell through to the fact-only fallback, which by the earlier fixes above is *intentionally* indistinguishable in shape from the plain evidence list.

Fixed at the ontology level, not by special-casing supply_chain in Python: a relationship type can now declare `causal: true` in its own YAML definition (`OntologyRegistry.causal_relationship_types()`); core.yaml's 5 generic types and supply_chain.yaml's 4 domain-specific ones for this exact chain now both carry the flag, and `GraphRepository._CAUSAL_RELATIONSHIP_TYPES` is computed from the registry instead of hardcoded — so a *future* domain pack controls whether its own relationship types participate in causal reasoning by flagging them in its own file, without a `graph_repository.py` code change. Covered by two new registry-level tests (`tests/test_ontology.py`) and one new orchestrator-level test using the exact worked-example relationship names (`tests/test_causal_recommendation.py`).

Separately investigated at the time, not a code bug — a real data gap, **since closed**: `solandra_supply_chain` and `large_scale_demo` (the two knowledge bases this worked example was built for) had **zero ingested entities** — the "Solandra Drive" connector errors ("no supported files found in that Drive folder"), so this exact example had never actually been ingested anywhere in this deployment. The causal-reasoning code itself was real and correctly wired (verified via the tests above, using synthetic data shaped exactly like the spec's example); it just had nothing real to run against.

**Follow-up, done — `solandra_supply_chain` now has real, live data across four connector types.** Ingested a real Solandra dataset (CSV ERP snapshots, a Gmail export, an Outlook export, and two crawled web pages) covering the exact narrative this worked example was written for: a CX-17 Power Relay quality defect at supplier Ferrotek Components, an at-risk order (SO-45821) for Vantus Robotics, an expedited-qualification decision routed through Meridian Circuit Supply, and the people/approvals around it. Current live counts (via the connector-scoped graph queries below): CSV ERP 49 entities/60 facts, Outlook 32/46, Gmail 17/23, plus two web-page connectors. All three upload-based connectors (CSV, Gmail, Outlook) were created under a throwaway tenant to unblock ingestion before the real tenant had `solandra_supply_chain` in its own `TENANT_API_KEYS` knowledge-base list, then consolidated into the real tenant via the new `reassign-tenant` admin route (see v1's newest follow-up) once that list was updated and redeployed — no re-ingestion needed for the consolidation itself. `solandra_drive`'s original mimeType-detection error (above) was never revisited; the CSV/Gmail/Outlook path made it moot for this dataset.

**Follow-up, done — exercised "Explain why + recommend" and plain Ask against this real data with 8 cross-source reasoning questions; 2/8 fully succeeded, and the failures surfaced three real, distinct, confirmed bugs (not data gaps):**

1. **The causal-chain walker can't cross a `PRODUCES`/`PROVIDES`/`TRIGGERED_BY` edge**, only `causal: true`-flagged types (`AFFECTS`/`DEPENDS_ON`/`CAUSED_BY`/`RESULTED_IN`/`SOURCED_FROM`, plus any domain pack's own flagged types). Real extracted data routes a defect's root cause through exactly these un-flagged types (`Ferrotek -PRODUCES-> lot -PRODUCES-> component`, `corrective action -TRIGGERED_BY-> defect`) — so a causal walk reaches the affected component but dead-ends one hop short of the actual root cause, even though the fact is fully present and well-connected in the graph. `TRIGGERED_BY` in particular is a strange omission given its name is literally causal language. Blocked reconstructing the full public-announcement-to-decision chain (question 8) for the same reason.
2. **The `:Decision` entity type is dual-purpose and the collision silently eats real data.** `ontology/core.yaml` defines `Decision` as a legitimate generic business entity ("a decision, approval, rejection, or recommendation") — but it's also the exact type `app/graph/decisions.py`'s `record_decision()` uses for Saxon's own internal causal-recommendation audit trail, and every retrieval path in this codebase explicitly excludes `:Decision`-labeled nodes (`NOT n:Decision`) to keep that internal audit trail out of normal answers. A real CSV named `decisions.csv` auto-infers record type "Decision" (`_infer_spec`'s stem-singularization) — an exact match — so a genuine client decision record (a $38,400 budget approval, `DEC-2026-014`) almost certainly got extracted correctly and then rendered permanently invisible everywhere, including to a query asking for it by its literal ID. Needs a real fix (e.g., namespacing Saxon's own audit-trail type distinctly from the ontology's general-purpose `Decision`) before any client dataset with real "decision" records can be trusted.
3. **A fact with no retrieved "transition partner" is silently dropped from the synthesized answer, producing a false "no matching graph context found."** `ContextOrchestrator`'s synthesis step (`app/context/orchestrator.py`, ~line 274-293) only includes `is_valid: true` facts as plain lines; an invalidated fact is only ever surfaced by pairing it with the newer fact that superseded it into a "changed from X to Y" transition line. If that newer fact isn't *also* present in the same retrieval batch, the invalidated-but-real fact is dropped entirely — not shown as a plain fact, not shown as a transition — even though `metadata.facts` (the raw, unfiltered evidence list) still has it. Hit repeatedly (3 of the 8 questions) whenever the only relevant facts happened to be superseded ones, including a case where the single dropped fact was itself the fully correct answer to the question asked.
4. **Possessive forms break entity-name matching.** "Ferrotek's" fails to resolve to "Ferrotek Components" and triggers the hard "no entity matching that name was found" short-circuit reserved for confident non-matches — confirmed by re-running the identical question with the possessive removed, which then resolved correctly. `_extract_candidate_entities`/`_normalize_entity_name` (`app/graph/entity_resolution.py`) don't strip a trailing `'s`.
5. **Descriptive/role-based references never resolve** ("the supplier who caused the defect" vs. "the one confirming the fix shipped") — expected, not a bug: entity resolution is name/ID-pattern matching only, so a question naming no proper entity falls through without attempting the two-way comparison it needs.

Two of the eight also showed real successes worth keeping in mind: a genuine multi-source join (an "Owen Whitfield, in order, looped in whom" question correctly pooled his own facts across CSV, Gmail, *and* Outlook with accurate per-fact source attribution) worked as designed, just without chronological ordering or explicitly flagging which steps lacked an email trail (both because the synthesis prompt doesn't ask for either) — and a negative-control question (whether Plant 1 was ever relevant) retrieved the exactly-correct single fact before hitting bug #3 above. One question's result (current Brightpeak account owner) was inconclusive — the connector's data had been purged and re-synced several times during the same session's incident recovery, muddying whether a wrong "owner not specified" answer reflects a real product bug or test-data churn; worth a clean re-test before drawing a conclusion.

**Follow-up, done — all four bugs above closed; audited 2026-09-03 to confirm none had regressed or been left half-fixed:**

1. **Causal walker crossing PRODUCES/TRIGGERED_BY: fixed.** Both carry `causal: true` in `ontology/core.yaml` (with comments explaining why, referencing this exact bug). `PROVIDES` was considered for the same flag during this audit and deliberately left alone — `tests/test_ontology.py::test_causal_relationship_types_includes_core_generic_types` asserts it stays non-causal on purpose ("administrative/classificatory rather than causal"), and the original bug report's own worked example never actually demonstrated a PROVIDES dead-end, only PRODUCES and TRIGGERED_BY.
2. **Decision entity collision: fixed.** `app/graph/decisions.py` tags every Saxon-generated recommendation with a second, Saxon-reserved `:SaxonRecommendation` label (plus a startup backfill migration for nodes created before the label existed) and every "hide Saxon's own audit trail" query in the codebase now filters on that narrower label, not on the ontology's general-purpose `:Decision` type — confirmed via a full-codebase search: no retrieval path filters on bare `:Decision` anymore. A real client `decisions.csv` record is retrievable like any other entity.
3. **Dropped invalidated fact with no transition partner: fixed.** `get_context_packet` (`app/context/orchestrator.py`) builds its summary from `_build_answer_lines`, which surfaces an unpaired invalidated fact as a "No longer current, but real: ..." line instead of silently dropping it — verified live against the real Ferrotek/CX-17 data this exact bug was originally found on.
4. **Possessive forms breaking entity matching: fixed.** `entity_resolution.py` strips a trailing possessive (ASCII `'s` and the Unicode `’` right single quote) before candidate extraction and again before the CONTAINS-fallback tier; "Ferrotek's" now resolves to "Ferrotek Components."

Separately, this session closed two more real, live-verified bugs not on the original eight-question list: the graph-visualization panel fetched nodes and relationships as two independent "most recent" queries, so on Solandra's real size the two barely overlapped and almost no edges ever rendered (summary said "187 relationships," the picture showed 2 lines) — fixed by building the displayed node set from the fetched relationships' own endpoints first (`frontend/app.js`'s `loadGraph`). And `_synthesize_answer`'s system prompt was allowing generic AI throat-clearing ("Based on the facts provided...") instead of answering directly — tightened without loosening any of its existing anti-hallucination/anti-conflation guarantees.

**Still open:** the Brightpeak Automation account-ownership question (Q7 above) is diagnosed but not yet remediated — repeated purge/resync testing during incident recovery left a stale wall-clock-timestamped extraction pass superseding the real, correctly-dated Outlook handoff fact. The fix (`DELETE /connectors/{id}/data` on the Solandra ERP (CSV) connector, then a forced re-sync) is scoped and approved but not yet executed — it's a real data-affecting action gated behind explicit interactive confirmation.

### Source authority on connectors

**Goal:** a way to break a tie when two sources disagree about the same fact at the same point in time (e.g. an ERP connector outranking a SharePoint doc on order status), without ever hiding or filtering a fact — provenance stays visible either way.

**Status: done.** `POST /api/v1/connectors` accepts an optional `source_authority` (0–100, default 0, higher wins), stored on `:Connector` (`app/graph/connectors.py`). `connectors.authority_by_group_id()` maps each of a tenant's group_ids to the max authority among the connectors feeding it. `ContextOrchestrator._apply_authority_tie_break` (`app/context/orchestrator.py`) runs only when two currently-valid facts share the same (source, target, relationship_type) key, disagree on the fact text, **and** actually come from more than one connector — in that case only, the higher-authority fact's line feeds the synthesized answer; **every** fact, winner or not, still comes back in `metadata.facts`, now tagged `source_authority`/`is_authoritative`, so a client can always see both sides of a disagreement even though only one drove the answer. Frontend: an optional authority input on the connector-creation form, and an "Authority N" badge on the connector table when set above 0.

**Follow-up, done — a real answer-truncation bug found against live northwind data, not a hypothetical:** the original (source, target)-only grouping key treated *any* two currently-valid facts about the same pair of nodes as a "disagreement," even when they weren't — e.g. an order's ship city and ship country are two different `LOCATED_AT` edges between the same two nodes, both true, from the same connector. That silently dropped one of them from the synthesized answer, which is exactly the "don't hide or filter" rule this field is supposed to honor. Fixed two ways: (1) the grouping key now includes `relationship_type` (added to `_entity_own_facts` and the semantic-search branch in `app/graph/graph_repository.py`, since it wasn't being carried on fact dicts before); (2) a group is only ever arbitrated when its facts actually span more than one `group_id` — arbitrating a connector against itself is never meaningful, since `source_authority` ranks *connectors* against each other, not individual facts. Also fixed: the "how is X connected to Y" path-lookup branch returns every hop with a blank `("", "")` source/target, which used to collide every hop of a multi-hop path into one shared group and collapse the whole answer to a single hop — path-lookup facts (no real uuids) are now excluded from grouping entirely. Covered by three new tests in `tests/test_source_authority.py`.

### v6 — BI as a first-class consumer

**Goal:** the reference architecture names Power BI alongside Copilots/agents/apps as things the same Context Layer should serve reusably — this repo already served the other three identically (chat UI + API, MCP for agents); Power BI wasn't a real fourth consumer yet, just a name on a slide.

**Status: done, via a read-only OData v4 feed rather than a certified custom Power BI connector.** `GET /api/v1/odata/*` (`app/api/odata.py`) exposes `Entities` and `Facts` as OData feeds (service document, `$metadata` CSDL, `$top`-capped listing), tenant-scoped by the same `X-API-Key` header/`require_tenant` dependency every other route uses. Power BI Desktop's built-in **Get Data → OData Feed** connector (Web API key auth) can point straight at this — no custom connector to build and submit through Microsoft's Partner Center certification process, which was judged out of proportion to what a client actually needs (a live, governed table Power BI can refresh against) versus what certification buys (marketplace discoverability this project doesn't need yet). Deliberately minimal — `$top` only, not `$filter`/`$orderby`/`$expand` — since the data source underneath is the exact same Cypher `app/api/graph.py` already runs for the UI; extend that if a report needs richer server-side filtering. A genuine custom Power BI connector (M/Power Query, `.pqx`, certification) remains the right answer if/when marketplace distribution becomes a real goal — this satisfies the pivot's actual requirement ("Power BI is a real fourth consumer") without that overhead.

No design call is open on this one — an OData feed vs. a certified custom connector is a build-cost/reach tradeoff, not a data-model or architecture decision, and the OData feed can coexist with a future custom connector rather than being replaced by one.
